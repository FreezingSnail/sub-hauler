Visual references for the first story beat

DeepSeek’s research shortlist, with source screenshots checked and collected locally for comparison. These are reference works, not proposed Sub-hauler artwork.

Back to the first-beat sketchbook

Our sequence stays: sub arrives → text → grow tanks → text → repair request → text.

Full 128×64 pictures. Separate text cards. No fully shown people in our artwork.

Most useful starting pair: Uninvited for deliberate room composition and material texture; Barotrauma for a submarine framed by dark water and a few lit spaces. Circuit Dude is the native-size reality check.

1. Uninvited

ICOM Simulations · screenshot hosted by Macintosh Repository.

Study
A readable room with distinct foreground, floor, wall and focal objects. Hatching follows surfaces rather than becoming uniform noise.
For our cards
Use a few clearly separated tanks, a lamp and connecting equipment. Let the room explain its purpose.
Scale limit
This screenshot includes a much larger display and interface. We need fewer objects and coarser texture. Its caption window is not our proposed layout: we keep separate text cards.

2. Dark Castle

Silicon Beach Software · screenshot hosted by Macintosh Repository.

Study
Large black and white areas establish structure before the small texture marks do any work.
For our cards
Separate the grow tanks from the room, or the pump parts from the workbench, using clear light and dark shapes first.
Scale limit
The tiny brickwork, figures and interface are not targets. Much of that detail would disappear at 128×64.
Source and screenshot gallery

3. Barotrauma

FakeFish / Undertow Games · official press screenshot.

Study
A strong submarine silhouette, a dark surrounding cavern, and light that makes the vessel feel inhabited.
For our arrival
One small sub approaching one lit Benthos berth. Keep the arrival readable before adding surface texture.
Scale limit
Composition and lighting reference only. The many rooms, color, crew and fine machinery cannot be reduced directly into a 128×64 card.
Official press page · Original screenshot
NATIVE-SIZE CHECK · 128×64

4. Circuit Dude

crait / Jonathan Holmes · Arduboy community screenshot.

Study
What a real 128×64 image can resolve: consistent edges, small repeated modules, and distinct shapes without grayscale.
For our repair request
Choose two recognizable mechanical subjects with clean contours. This is a pixel-budget check, not a proposal to use a puzzle-board composition.
Scale limit
This is the only downloaded reference here at our exact screen size. Even so, its object density is too high for a single narrative focal point.
Developer’s community post · Original screenshot

5. Space Haven

Bugbyte · official simulated-gas-system screenshot.

Study
Equipment arranged as a working room: repeated bays, access aisles and connections that suggest what each space does.
For our grow tanks
Arrange two or three plant tanks as part of a practical life-support room, with one clear filter or pipe connection.
Scale limit
This specific screenshot shows machinery and gas overlays, not a clean grow-tank illustration. Use it for spatial organization; its isometric detail, labels, color and crew are not our style target.
